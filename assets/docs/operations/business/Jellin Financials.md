# Jellin — Financials

This document outlines the Jellin platform's cost structure, pricing model, and revenue streams.

---

## 1. Operating Costs

### 1.1 Infrastructure

The Jellin application is hosted on **Microsoft Azure**. Azure infrastructure constitutes the primary ongoing operating cost, covering compute, storage, networking, and managed services.

### 1.2 Team

| Role | Count | Scope |
|------|-------|-------|
| Application Engineer | 1 | Development and maintenance of the Jellin platform (mobile apps, backend, business app) |
| Application Engineer / Product & BD | 1 | Platform engineering, product strategy, business development, and go-to-market execution |

Both founding team members are engineers. The second engineer also serves as the product and business development lead.

### 1.3 Development Investment

The cost of initial application development — including the platform itself, the website, and all marketing and business development materials — has been funded through **stakeholder contributions**. This investment is treated as founding capital, not as a recurring operating expense.

## 2. Pricing Model

Jellin uses a tiered subscription model with a trial period, a standard plan, and an enterprise tier.

### 2.1 Trial Period

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 1 month |
| **Cost** | Free |
| **Features** | All modules active |
| **Limits** | Standard usage limits apply |

New businesses get full access to the platform for one month at no cost. All modules are enabled during the trial to allow businesses to experience the complete Jellin feature set before committing.

### 2.2 Standard Plan

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price** | $20 / month |
| **Features** | All modules active |
| **Group limit** | Maximum 5 groups |

The standard plan includes everything Jellin offers — loyalty programs, all three coupon types (counting, instant, sweepstake), check-in methods, user management, cross-business collaboration, multi-channel delivery, and full analytics.

The only constraint is a maximum of **5 groups**, which is sufficient for most small-to-medium businesses operating a handful of locations or partner relationships.

### 2.3 Enterprise Plan

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price** | $150 – $2,500 / month (estimated) |
| **Features** | All modules active |
| **Group limit** | Unlimited (based on agreement) |
| **Sales model** | Contact sales |

The enterprise plan is designed for large businesses, chains, franchises, malls, and multi-brand networks that require more than 5 groups. Pricing is determined through a **contact sales** engagement and scales based on the size and number of groups requested by the enterprise customer.

Enterprise pricing factors include:
- Number of groups (points of sale, partner networks)
- Scale of cross-business collaboration
- Volume of loyalty programs and active campaigns

### 2.4 Pricing Summary

| Plan | Price | Modules | Group Limit | Sales Model |
|------|-------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Trial | Free (1 month) | All | Standard | Self-service |
| Standard | $20 / month | All | Up to 5 | Self-service |
| Enterprise | $150 – $2,500 / month | All | Unlimited | Contact sales |

| | |

## 7. Jellin Reach Estimates by Vertical

This section estimates Jellin's realistic subscriber reach in each vertical over a 3-year horizon, based on the use cases and personas described in the [Jellin Personas](Jellin%20Personas.md) document. Estimates assume Greece-only deployment with the current pricing model.

### 7.1 Cafés & Coffee Shops — "The Daily Regular" Scenario

**Persona alignment:** Sarah (The Daily Regular), The Independent Shop Owner

**Use case:** "Free coffee every 5 visits" counting coupon. Phone-number-only enrollment. Real-time analytics on repeat visit rates.

| Timeframe | Target Subscribers | Penetration Rate | Estimated MRR |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|---------------|
| Year 1 | 200 – 400 | 0.6% – 1.1% | €4,000 – €8,000 |
| Year 2 | 800 – 1,500 | 2.3% – 4.3% | €16,000 – €30,000 |
| Year 3 | 2,000 – 3,500 | 5.7% – 10.0% | €40,000 – €70,000 |

**Rationale:** Cafés have the lowest barrier to adoption — single-location operators, simple loyalty logic, and high customer frequency. The zero-friction sign-up (phone number only) is a perfect fit for the fast-paced café environment. Word-of-mouth effects are strong in café clusters (commercial streets, neighborhoods). Most café operators are on the **Standard plan ($20/mo)**.

### 7.2 Apparel & Fashion Retail — "The Mall Explorer" Scenario

**Persona alignment:** The Mall Explorer, The Independent Shop Owner, The Ecosystem Builder

**Use case:** Cross-store mall-wide rewards (earn Jells at fashion store, redeem at bookstore). Instant coupons tied to seasonal collections. Partner networks between complementary brands.

| Timeframe | Target Subscribers | Penetration Rate | Estimated MRR |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|---------------|
| Year 1 | 50 – 150 | 0.3% – 1.0% | €1,000 – €3,000 |
| Year 2 | 300 – 700 | 2.0% – 4.7% | €6,000 – €14,000 |
| Year 3 | 800 – 1,500 | 5.3% – 10.0% | €16,000 – €30,000 |

**Rationale:** Fashion retail has longer purchase cycles than cafés, making counting-based loyalty (visits) and instant coupons (seasonal discounts) both relevant. Mall-based apparel stores are natural candidates for ecosystem-level deals (Enterprise plan). Independent boutiques on commercial streets adopt the Standard plan for customer retention during off-season months.

### 7.3 Gyms & Fitness Centers — "The Fitness Enthusiast" Scenario

**Persona alignment:** The Fitness Enthusiast, The Multi-Location Operator

**Use case:** QR-based check-in across multiple gym locations. Accumulated Jells unlock free personal training sessions or merchandise. Unified dashboard for chain operators.

| Timeframe | Target Subscribers | Penetration Rate | Estimated MRR |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|---------------|
| Year 1 | 30 – 80 | 1.5% – 4.0% | €600 – €1,600 |
| Year 2 | 120 – 300 | 6.0% – 15.0% | €2,400 – €6,000 |
| Year 3 | 250 – 500 | 12.5% – 25.0% | €5,000 – €10,000 |

**Rationale:** Smaller total addressable market but higher adoption rate potential. Multi-location gym chains are natural Enterprise plan customers ($150–$2,500/mo) due to their need for unified cross-location tracking and employee performance monitoring. The QR check-in model aligns perfectly with existing gym workflows. Few competitors offer loyalty specifically designed for fitness networks.

**Note:** While subscriber counts are lower, average revenue per subscriber is higher due to the Enterprise plan mix. Estimated blended ARPU: ~€30–50/mo vs. €20/mo for cafés.

### 7.4 Hotels & Hospitality — "The Hotel Traveler" Scenario

**Persona alignment:** The Hotel Traveler, The Ecosystem Builder, The Multi-Location Operator

**Use case:** Concierge-initiated Jells at check-in. Welcome SMS with partner discounts (nearby cafés, boutiques, restaurants). Cross-business loyalty ecosystem around the hotel property.

| Timeframe | Target Subscribers | Penetration Rate | Estimated MRR |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|---------------|
| Year 1 | 20 – 60 | 0.5% – 1.4% | €1,500 – €10,000 |
| Year 2 | 80 – 200 | 1.9% – 4.7% | €6,000 – €40,000 |
| Year 3 | 200 – 500 | 4.7% – 11.6% | €15,000 – €100,000 |

**Rationale:** Hotels represent the highest revenue-per-subscriber opportunity. The ecosystem builder model — where a hotel enrolls partner businesses (restaurants, cafés, boutiques, tour operators) into a shared loyalty umbrella — generates multiple subscriptions per deal. A single 4-star hotel ecosystem can drive 3–8 partner subscriptions plus the hotel's own Enterprise plan. The wide MRR range reflects the high variance in deal sizes. Seasonal tourism patterns affect adoption timing (peak onboarding: Q1–Q2 before summer season).

**Note:** CRM/ERP connector add-ons ($70/connector/year) are most likely in this vertical, as hotels commonly use PMS (Property Management Systems) that benefit from Jellin integration.

### 7.5 Shopping Malls — "The Mall Explorer" Scenario

**Persona alignment:** The Mall Explorer, The Ecosystem Builder, The Marketing Professional

**Use case:** Mall-wide rewards program where Jells earned at any tenant store accumulate toward a universal gift card. White-label marketplace under the mall's brand. Shared foot traffic analytics across tenants.

| Timeframe | Target Subscribers | Penetration Rate | Estimated MRR |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|---------------|
| Year 1 | 2 – 5 malls | 7% – 17% of major malls | €1,000 – €12,500 |
| Year 2 | 5 – 10 malls | 17% – 33% | €2,500 – €25,000 |
| Year 3 | 8 – 15 malls | 27% – 50% | €4,000 – €37,500 |

**Rationale:** Very small number of targets but very high contract value. Each mall deal is an Enterprise plan ($500–$2,500/mo) and creates a captive ecosystem where individual tenant stores also onboard. A single mall deployment can generate 20–50 additional Standard-plan tenant subscriptions (partially overlapping with café and apparel vertical counts). Mall operators are motivated by foot traffic analytics and cross-tenant engagement — capabilities that differentiate Jellin from standard loyalty card programs.

### 8.8 Assumptions & Notes

1. **Revenue recognition**: Actual collected revenue uses linear MRR ramp (average of start-of-year and end-of-year MRR × 12). End-of-year ARR is listed separately as a run-rate indicator.
2. **Personnel**: Greek employment law mandates 14-month pay (13th + 14th salary). Employer social security contributions at ~25%. Loaded cost includes all statutory obligations. The founding team consists of 2 engineers.
3. **Azure scaling**: Variable costs are conservative estimates. Actual costs may be lower with reserved instances, spot pricing, and Azure startup credits (BizSpark/Founders Hub — up to $150K in free Azure credits, not factored in).
4. **LLM support**: Based on GPT-4o-class API pricing as of early 2026. Costs are expected to decrease 30–50% year-over-year as model efficiency improves and competition increases. Projections use current rates without deflation adjustment — actuals may be materially lower.
5. **Marketing**: Assumes Greece-only digital marketing. Cost-per-click in the Greek market (~€0.15–€0.40 on Meta, ~€0.30–€0.80 on Google) is significantly lower than Western European/US benchmarks.
6. **No external funding**: Projections assume operations are self-funded after the initial stakeholder development investment. No VC rounds, grants, or debt included.
7. **No churn adjustment**: Subscriber counts are gross additions. Applying a 10% annual churn rate would reduce Year 3 net subscribers by ~15–20% and revenue proportionally.
8. **Tax**: All figures are pre-tax. Greek corporate income tax rate is 22%. Greek startup incentives may apply (e.g., tax credits for R&D and innovation under Law 4712/2020).
9. **Currency**: Pricing is in USD (per Section 2) but costs are in EUR. An implicit EUR/USD rate of ~1:1 is used for simplicity. Exchange rate fluctuations could impact margins by ±3–5%.
